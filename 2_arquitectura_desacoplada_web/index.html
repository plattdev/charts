Matching Chart Geometry to What the Data Says

Each public dataset has its own shape—multivariate trade-offs, regional distributions, 50-year transitions, physical areas, threshold shares, or multi-stage flows. Rather than forcing one standard template, I experimented with selecting a different chart type for each dataset depending on the structure of its numbers and the question it answers.

SCATTER PLOT

Which countries convert their economic resources into actual human well-being and sustainability?

Comparing 140 nations across Sustainable Development, World Happiness, and GDP per capita (PPP).

What the data shows: Mid-income nations such as Costa Rica achieve top-tier happiness (7.44/10) and strong SDG progress (73.6/100) at a fraction of high-income GDP, showing that beyond ~$30k PPP per capita, extra wealth yields diminishing returns in human well-being.

Methodology

Records are harmonized across 140 countries with complete coverage in all three international datasets. Subjective well-being uses the World Happiness Report Cantril ladder 3-year rolling average (2022–2024) to reduce annual survey sampling noise; sustainability uses the normalized Sustainable Development Report index across all 17 UN SDGs; and economic output uses the latest available World Bank GDP per capita adjusted for Purchasing Power Parity in constant 2021 international dollars, with marker radii scaled by square root (r ∝ √GDP) so circle area stays proportional to wealth.

Data Sources:
  • World Bank (GDP per capita, PPP)
  • Sustainable Development Report 2026
  • World Happiness Report

BEESWARM PLOT

How does Healthy Life Expectancy (HALE) at birth vary across world regions?

Comparing 185 countries & territories across 6 WHO regions from 2000 to 2023 (latest official WHO Global Health Estimates).

What the data shows: Global healthy life expectancy climbed by 4.9 years from 2000 to 2023 (from 57.9 to 62.8 years, rebounding after the 2021 pandemic dip), yet a ~14-year gap still separates the highest- and lowest-performing WHO regions.

Sex
Metric

Methodology

Healthy Life Expectancy (HALE) at birth is estimated via the WHO Global Health Estimates Sullivan method, combining standard abridged life tables with age- and sex-specific disability prevalence weights from the Global Burden of Disease study across 185 member states (2000–2023). Regional diamonds reflect official WHO population-weighted aggregates, while country dots retain exact values and resolve density collisions via deterministic circle-tangent packing.

Data Sources:
  • WHO — HALE at birth (WHOSIS_000002)
  • WHO Global Health Observatory OData API

STREAMGRAPH

How did Sweden shift 50 years of municipal waste away from landfill?

Continuous 50-year evolution of Swedish municipal waste (1975–2025) across 6 treatment pathways in metric tonnes (Source: Avfall Sverige).

What the data shows: Between 1975 and 2025, Sweden slashed municipal landfilling from 62.3% (1.62M tonnes) down to just 1.5% (67k tonnes) by steadily expanding energy recovery, material recycling, and biological treatment.

Baseline Mode

Methodology

Historical records from Avfall Sverige (1975–2025) are harmonized in metric tonnes across six standardized municipal waste treatment categories, combining five-year benchmark intervals prior to 1995 with annual series thereafter. Stacked series are computed under centered silhouette, 100% proportional, or zero-baseline geometry and interpolated with Fritsch–Carlson monotone cubic splines to prevent artificial curve overshoots between observations.

Data Sources:
  • Avfall Sverige (1975–2019)
  • Swedish Waste Management 2024 (PDF)
  • Avfallstrenden 2024 (PDF)

PROPORTIONAL AREAS

How much has the Antarctic ozone hole shrunk since its historical peak?

Area-scaled comparison of Antarctic ozone hole extent (1979–2025) in million km² and minimum column ozone in Dobson Units (DU) (Source: NASA Ozone Watch).

What the data shows: Since peaking at 26.6 million km² in 2006, the Antarctic ozone hole has contracted by nearly 30% to 18.7 million km² in 2025, tracking gradual stratospheric recovery under the Montreal Protocol.

Comparison

Methodology

Annual values derive from NASA Goddard Space Flight Center satellite total-column ozone observations (TOMS, OMI, and OMPS instruments, 1979–2025). Following NASA Ozone Watch standard seasonal windows, annual hole extent averages daily areas falling below 220 DU between September 7 and October 13, while minimum column ozone averages daily lows from September 21 to October 16. Semicircle radii are computed as r = Rmax × √(A / Amax) so rendered surface area scales linearly with square kilometers.

Data Source:
  • NASA Ozone Watch — Goddard Space Flight Center (1979–2025)

WAFFLE CHART

What share of the world’s 996 largest cities cross extreme climate hazard thresholds as warming rises?

Three 10 × 10 grids comparing +1.5°C, +2.0°C, and +3.0°C global warming across 996 cities (>500k residents, ~2.1B people). Each square equals 1% of major cities (Source: World Resources Institute, 2024).

What the data shows: Moving from +1.5°C to +3.0°C of global warming multiplies the share of major cities facing extreme heat more than sevenfold—jumping from 6.0% (60 cities) to 45.4% (452 cities).

Extreme threshold: ≥ 60 days/yr above local 95th percentile

Methodology

Covers all 996 urban centers exceeding 500,000 inhabitants in the Global Human Settlement Layer (GHSL) Urban Centre Database. Following WRI’s Technical Note methodology, indicator projections use each city’s top-ranked bias-adjusted NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 climate model (model_rank = 1, calibrated against ERA5 historical reanalysis) evaluated over 10-year windows centered on +1.5°C, +2.0°C, and +3.0°C above preindustrial (1880–1900) temperatures, counting the percentage of cities that cross indicator-specific extreme thresholds.

Data Sources:
  • World Resources Institute — City-Scale Climate Hazard Indicators (2024)
  • WRI Technical Note (DOI: 10.46830/writn.23.00154)

SANKEY PLOT

What drove 62.3 million internal displacements worldwide in 2025, and which regions bore the impact?

3-stage flow of global internal displacements (flows, 2020–2025) from Primary Cause (Conflict vs. Disaster) through Hazard Type into 7 World Regions (Source: IDMC Global Internal Displacement Database).

What the data shows: In 2025, armed conflict (32.4M) and weather-related disasters (29.9M)—led by storms and floods—drove 62.3 million internal displacements globally, concentrated in East Asia & Pacific, Sub-Saharan Africa, and MENA.

Reporting Year

Methodology

Aggregates annual event-level internal displacement flows (movements recorded January 1 – December 31) from the IDMC Global Internal Displacement Database rather than year-end IDP stocks, preserving event attribution to specific triggers. Conflict records without a natural hazard code are mapped to an explicit Stage 2 Conflict & Violence node to conserve 100% of volume across all three stages; minor natural triggers beyond the top five hazards are grouped into Other Disasters; and Stage 3 destination regions are ordered by weighted source barycenter to minimize ribbon crossings.

Data Source:
  • IDMC — Global Internal Displacement Database (GIDD, 2020–2025)
