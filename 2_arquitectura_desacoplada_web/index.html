SCATTER PLOT

Which countries convert their economic resources into actual human well-being and sustainability?

Comparing 140 nations across Sustainable Development, World Happiness, and GDP per capita (PPP).

About This Visualization & Methodology

Core Question: Which countries are highly efficient at converting their economic resources into actual human well-being and sustainability, and which wealthy nations are underperforming?

X-Axis: Sustainable Development Index (0–100): Normalized score measuring progress across all 17 UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) from the Sustainable Development Report.

Y-Axis: World Happiness Index (0–10): National subjective well-being averaged over 3 survey years (2022–2024) from the World Happiness Report.

Bubble Area: GDP per Capita (PPP 2021 $): Purchasing Power Parity at constant 2021 international dollars, square-root scaled so bubble surface accurately conveys wealth volume.

About This Beeswarm Plot & Methodology

What is HALE? Healthy Life Expectancy (HALE) at birth measures the average number of years a newborn is expected to live in full health, subtracting years lived with disability or ill health from overall life expectancy.

6 WHO Regions (Colorblind-Safe Palette): Countries and territories are grouped into their official World Health Organization regions using the Okabe-Ito colorblind-safe palette. Diamonds mark the official aggregate regional average.

Collision-Free Beeswarm: Each dot’s horizontal coordinate shows its exact value, while vertical offsets are calculated via circle-tangent packing so high-density peaks spread without overlapping.

2000–2023 Recovery: Worldwide, healthy life expectancy improved by +4.9 years from 57.9 years in 2000 to 62.8 years in 2023, recovering steadily from the 2021 pandemic dip (61.4 years).

About This Streamgraph & Methodology

50 Years of Waste Transition: Between 1975 and 2025, Sweden reduced municipal landfill from 1.62M tonnes (62.3%) to 67,370 tonnes (1.5%) while scaling energy recovery, material recycling, and biological treatment.

Why a Streamgraph? Unlike discrete year-to-category diagrams, a continuous streamgraph preserves the chronological time axis (1975–2025) and lets readers visually trace how each treatment pathway expands or contracts over five decades.

Monotone Cubic Curves & Colorblind-Safe Palette: Band edges are interpolated using Fritsch-Carlson monotone cubic splines (preventing artificial overshoots) and colored with an Okabe-Ito-harmonized palette. Hover any band or legend item to isolate a single pathway.

Three Baselines: Switch between the centered Streamgraph (default), 100% Share to compare relative composition, or Stacked Area to read exact total tonnage from a flat zero baseline.

About This Proportional Areas Chart & Methodology

Why Proportional Areas? Because ozone hole extent is literally measured as a surface area in million km², encoding area with area mirrors satellite views of the polar vortex while letting readers judge recovery at a single glance.

Scaled by Area, Never by Radius: Each semicircle radius is computed as r = Rmax × √(A / Amax). Dividing the radius directly would shrink the visual area by a factor of 4 and exaggerate changes; square-root scaling guarantees area truthfulness.

Center-Aligned Step & Geography Anchor: Adjoining both area halves along a shared vertical axis creates an instantaneous visual step. Concentric reference rings for Antarctica (14.2M km²) and the 1979 pre-hole baseline (0.1M km²) ground abstract figures in real geography.

NASA Seasonal Windows & Decadal Trends: Antarctic winter stratospheric temperatures cause natural meteorological swings. Comparing key benchmarks (2006 peak vs. 2025 or 5-year means) isolates Montreal Protocol progress.

About This Waffle Chart & Methodology

Why a 3-Panel 10 × 10 Waffle Chart? Turning percentages into discrete 1% units makes the jump from the +1.5°C Paris Agreement target to +2.0°C and +3.0°C tangible at a single glance—no axis estimation required.

996 Major Cities (~2.1 Billion Residents): Encompasses every urban center worldwide with over 500,000 inhabitants in the Global Human Settlement Layer (GHSL) Urban Centre Database, representing 56% of the global urban population tracked in GHSL.

Best-Fit Downscaled Climate Models: Following WRI’s Technical Note methodology, values use each city’s top-ranked NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 model (model_rank = 1, calibrated against ERA5 reanalysis) across 10-year windows centered on +1.5°C, +2.0°C, and +3.0°C above preindustrial (1880–1900) temperatures.

Colorblind-Safe Progression: Filled squares step through amber (#E69F00), vermillion (#D55E00), and deep crimson (#9E2A2B) with distinct luminance levels, ensuring clear visual separation for all color vision types.

About This Sankey Plot & Methodology

Internal Displacements (Flows) vs. IDPs (Stocks): Following IDMC methodology, this Sankey aggregates Internal Displacements—annual population displacement movements recorded between January 1 and December 31—rather than year-end IDP stocks, preserving event-level attribution to specific hazards and regions.

Continuous 3-Stage Flow (Conflict & Violence): Because conflict records do not carry a natural hazard classification in GIDD, blank hazard cells for conflict rows are explicitly mapped to Conflict & Violence in Stage 2 so the full 100% flow remains unbroken across all three stages.

Long-Tail Grouping & Crossing Minimization: The five largest disaster hazards (Storm, Flood, Earthquake, Wildfire, Drought) are shown individually while smaller hazards (mixed disasters, erosion, mass movement, volcanic activity, extreme temperature) roll into Other Disasters. Stage 3 regions are ordered by weighted source barycenter to minimize ribbon crossings.

Colorblind-Safe Encoding: Ribbons inherit their Stage 2 trigger hue—muted terracotta (#B84A39) for conflict, steel blue (#2A6F8E) for storms, deep teal (#2B8270) for floods, plum-slate (#7B6D8D) for earthquakes, ochre-orange (#D97724) for wildfires, and amber (#D49B2A) for drought—while destination regions use neutral slate bars (#334155) so readers can trace each driver cleanly.

Data Source:
  • Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre (IDMC) — Global Internal Displacement Database (GIDD), Disaggregated Flows (2020–2025)
